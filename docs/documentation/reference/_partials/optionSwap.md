**Example: Wrath & Ruin.** The Berserker's feature reads "Whenever you perform a notable act of destruction or feat of strength during a Safe Rest, you may choose different Berserker options available to you."

- **Label** → `Whenever you perform a notable act of destruction or feat of strength during a Safe Rest`
- **Option pools** → `all`
- **Offered on** → `Safe Rest`

A Berserker who holds this feature now sees a "Change my options" row in the safe rest window. Expanding it lists every pool the class has given them picks from, with their current picks already selected, and they may choose differently before confirming the rest. Closing the window without resting discards the changes.

Each feature that carries this rule gets its own card in the rest window, with the pools its rule covers inside it. The feature's own description is shown on hover and is never checked. The system cannot know whether a notable act of destruction happened, and it does not try: it offers the swap and the table decides whether it was earned. The Shadowmancer's Supplicate is the clearest case, since the swap there is a request its Patron may refuse, and may charge for.

Use `all` for the option pools unless the feature really does name a narrower set. The class features print "different *\<Class\>* options available to you" without naming a pool, so `all` is the faithful setting. Naming pools explicitly (`savage-arsenal`, `combat-tactics`) restricts the swap to those, and several rules on one character combine, with the widest winning.

A level that offers a choice between alternatives, where one alternative grants an item outright instead of drawing from a pool, folds every alternative into one pool: the granted item becomes a member the player can trade for a pool pick and back. If the grant rule on that alternative has **Allow duplicates on the actor** on, the member is *repeatable*: a character may hold more than one pick of it, and the swap keeps offering it while they already hold one. The Commander's +1 Max Combat Die is authored this way. Every other member may be held once, which is what "choose a 2nd ability" means, and the flag is off unless set. Pools that share a group are merged, so a pick never counts in two pools.

A pick is an item on the sheet whose source is a member of a pool, however it got there. Two items of the same option are two picks, shown as two places, and each can be traded on its own. The class level decides how many picks each pool grants, and the window shows the number chosen against the number granted. A one-for-one trade is always allowed. A shortfall can be filled in the window. An excess is reported under the pool and never blocked. The window does not compare the picks with the wording of each level, so a trade across the lists a class names is allowed without comment. The one rule the swap enforces is the book's "choose another": a member is offered a second time only when its grant allows duplicates.

The swap covers class option pools only. It never touches a subclass, an ability score increase, or spells known, because those choices are not recorded in a way that can be undone. For moving a skill point on a rest, use **Skill Point Move** instead.
